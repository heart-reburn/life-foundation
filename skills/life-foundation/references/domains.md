# The eight areas

Each area lists what it covers, signs it needs work, and starter practices. Starter practices are small, one-week experiments with a visible result.

## 1. Body (base layer)

**Covers:** sleep, movement, food, stress and recovery.

**Signs:** tired most days; sleep under 7 hours on most nights; little or no exercise; energy crashes; stress that doesn't settle.

**Starter practices:**
- Log bedtime, wake time, and how rested you feel for 14 days. Most adults need 7 or more hours (Watson et al., 2015).
- Set a fixed wake time for one week, including weekends.
- Add two 30-minute brisk walks this week. The adult guideline is 150-300 minutes of moderate activity a week (Bull et al., 2020).
- Write down three things that reliably calm you down, and use one on a stressful day.

## 2. Self-regulation

**Covers:** attention, emotions, habits, energy across the day.

**Signs:** goals exist but don't turn into action; easily distracted; reactions you regret; habits that don't stick.

**Starter practices:**
- Write one if-then plan for a habit ("When I sit down at my desk, I will open the task list first") and log each day it fires (Gollwitzer, 1999).
- One focused block a day with the phone in another room. Log how long it lasted.
- After a strong emotion, write the situation, the feeling, and one other way to see the situation (reappraisal; Gross, 1998).
- Track energy (high, medium, low) three times a day for a week to find your best hours.

## 3. Thinking

**Covers:** reasoning, spotting biases, noticing and learning from your own mistakes.

**Signs:** the same mistakes keep happening; strong opinions that turn out wrong; hard to explain why you believe something.

**Starter practices:**
- Weekly mistake log: what went wrong, what you assumed, what you'd check next time.
- Before a strong conclusion, write the best argument against it.
- Predict an outcome with a percentage, then check it later. Track how often you're right.

## 4. Decisions and priorities

**Covers:** choosing what matters, saying no, judging risk and uncertainty.

**Signs:** too many priorities and nothing gets depth; decisions feel like gut calls; you revisit the same choice over and over.

**Starter practices:**
- Write down your top three priorities for the month. Name one thing you will stop doing.
- Before a big decision, run a pre-mortem: imagine it failed a year from now and list why (Klein, 2007).
- Set a stop rule for one project: "If X hasn't happened by date Y, I stop."
- Keep a decision journal: the choice, what you expected, and the result later.

## 5. Understanding people

**Covers:** empathy, listening, reading situations, handling conflict.

**Signs:** conflicts escalate before you understand them; you miss what the other person needed; after conversations you think "I should have asked..."

**Starter practices:**
- After one tense conversation, write their side: the facts as they see them, how they felt, what they needed, what they asked for.
- In one conversation a day, summarize what the other person said before replying.
- Ask one more question than usual before giving an opinion.

## 6. Influence and communication

**Covers:** explaining clearly, persuading, asking for what you need, writing.

**Signs:** good ideas don't get buy-in; people misunderstand your requests; you avoid asking.

**Starter practices:**
- Explain one idea to its intended audience in under two minutes, then ask them to say it back.
- Rewrite one important message so the request is in the first sentence.
- Make one clear request this week that you'd normally avoid.

## 7. Problem-solving and execution

**Covers:** breaking problems down, finding the first step, iterating, finishing.

**Signs:** big problems stall; lots of starting, little finishing; stuck on the same problem for weeks.

**Starter practices:**
- For one stuck problem, write the next physical action, and do it within 24 hours.
- Restate one problem three different ways before solving it (Pólya, 1945).
- Ship a rough first version of something this week, then improve it.

## 8. Resources

**Covers:** money, time, tools, information, and people you can call on.

**Signs:** money stress; never enough time; reinventing things that already exist; not knowing who to ask.

**Starter practices:**
- Track every expense for two weeks without changing anything, then look for one surprise.
- Track time in 30-minute blocks for three days and compare it with your priorities.
- List five people you could ask for help, and what each is good at.
- Before starting a task, spend five minutes looking for an existing tool or template.
